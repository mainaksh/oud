🪷 Cultural & Historical Significance

In Khmer culture, agarwood (ឈើក្រអូប — Chheu Kror-Ob, meaning "fragrant wood") has been treasured since the Angkorian Empire (802-1431 CE). Ancient inscriptions at Angkor Wat reference aromatic woods burned during royal ceremonies. The Khmer Rouge era (1975-1979) devastated traditional knowledge, but since the 1990s, Cambodia has emerged as a premier oud source. Today, sustainable harvesting initiatives work with indigenous Bunong and Jarai communities to preserve both forests and cultural practices.

🐉 Cultural & Historical Significance

Vietnamese Trầm Hương (沉香 — "sinking fragrance") has been treasured since the Champa Kingdom (192-1832 CE). Ancient Chinese records praise "Champa agarwood" as the finest in the world. The legendary Song Dynasty poet Su Shi wrote odes to Vietnamese oud. During the Nguyễn Dynasty, agarwood was a royal monopoly, and the finest pieces were reserved for imperial ceremonies. Today, Vietnam's oud heritage continues through generations of master craftsmen who maintain traditional distillation methods passed down for centuries.

🇯🇵 The Japan Connection — 香道 (Kōdō)

Japanese incense masters have prized Vietnamese agarwood for over 1,000 years. In the refined art of Kōdō (香道 — "The Way of Fragrance"), Vietnamese oud is classified among the highest grades. Guiding Principles

The Ten Commandments of Supreme Oud

Fundamental principles that define excellence in oud oil production and evaluation

I

Thou Shalt Honor the Source

Only wild-harvested or properly aged plantation Aquilaria (minimum 30 years) with natural infection (minimum 10 years) shall be used for supreme grade production.

II

Thou Shalt Not Adulterate

No synthetic compounds, carrier oils, or extenders shall be added. Purity must be absolute and verifiable through advanced testing.

III

Thou Shalt Practice Patience

Proper soaking (minimum 21 days), slow distillation, and adequate aging (minimum 12 months) are non-negotiable for supreme quality.

IV

Thou Shalt Respect the Art

Distillation temperature shall never exceed 105°C. Slow heating (≤2°C/hour) preserves delicate aromatic compounds.

V

Thou Shalt Collect the Heart

Only the first heart fraction (Days 4-14) qualifies for supreme grade classification. Later fractions shall be graded separately.

VI

Thou Shalt Verify Authenticity

SNIF-NMR and/or IRMS testing is required to verify natural origin and geographic authenticity for supreme grade certification.

VII

Thou Shalt Document Everything

Complete traceability from source to final product, including CITES permits, chain of custody, and all test results must be maintained.

VIII

Thou Shalt Trust the Nose

Expert sensory evaluation is essential. A minimum score of 90/100 from qualified evaluators is required for supreme grade.

IX

Thou Shalt Protect Biodiversity

Sustainable harvesting practices and CITES compliance are moral imperatives, not merely legal requirements.

X

Thou Shalt Never Compromise

If any criterion is not met, the oil cannot be classified as supreme grade. There are no exceptions to excellence.
